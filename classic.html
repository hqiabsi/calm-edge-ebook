Chapter one

The attention audit

Before you change anything about how you work, you need to see it clearly. Not the version you describe in interviews — the version that actually happened last Tuesday. So this chapter asks for one thing: twenty minutes, a piece of paper, and an honest account of where your day went.

Most people guess wrong. Ask someone how much of their day is spent on focused work and they will say three or four hours. Ask them to log it for three days and the number is usually under ninety minutes, broken into fragments of eleven or twelve. The gap between the guess and the log is not a character flaw. It is simply what happens when a day is designed by other people's urgency.

You cannot fix an attention problem you cannot see. The audit is not about judgement — it is about making the invisible visible.

Here is the audit. Divide a page into columns for the hours you work. Every thirty minutes, write one word for what you were doing and one word for what you were supposed to be doing. That is all. No app, no timer, no categories to learn. At the end of three days, look for two patterns: the moments where intention and action matched, and the moments where they drifted.

The drift is where the information lives. Perhaps your best hour is being spent in a stand-up. Perhaps the two hours you block for writing are the two hours your team needs you most. Perhaps you are not short of time at all — you are short of uninterrupted time, which is a different problem with a different fix.

One warning. The first day of the audit usually feels terrible. You will notice how often you reach for your phone, how many times you re-read the same paragraph, how much of the day is reaction rather than choice. That discomfort is the point. It is also temporary. By the third day, most people find one or two genuinely good hours hiding in plain sight — hours that could be protected with a small change instead of a total life overhaul.

Write down that one change before you turn the page. Not five. One. The rest of this book works only if you start from something real.
